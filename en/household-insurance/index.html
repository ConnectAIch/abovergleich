    What drives the premium

    • Same excess: With a CHF 200 excess and CHF 5m liability, the basic packages cost CHF 296.40 (Smile), 363.30 (Helvetia) and 376.60 (Generali). Zurich and Mobiliar only offered the CHF 200 excess in their bigger package (CHF 451.90 and 520.91, with theft away from home and glass).
    • Package: At Mobiliar, the same flat costs CHF 230.58 (Minima), 520.91 (Classic) or 719.88 (Comfort). Add-ons are the biggest lever.
    • Sum insured: AXA suggested CHF 53'900, which brings the package down to CHF 319.00 from 360.65. If you are underinsured, though, claims get cut.
    • Liability alone: CHF 77.60 (Smile) to 92.03 (Mobiliar) a year.
    • Term: Some calculators suggest 2 or 5 years. After three years, any contract can be cancelled with 3 months' notice.

    Insurers earn more when you add every extra. Here is our honest view, based on the claims data.

    Personal liability insurance
    One accident can cost millions. 51% of all liability claims are damage to rented property, and as a tenant you are almost certainly affected. Largest single claim: over CHF 2m.
    ✓

    Fire, water, burglary, natural hazards: the basics. CHF 85'000 of cover cost CHF 12 to 25 a month in our test, without liability. Everything in your home is worth more than you think.

    ~

    Liability in detail

    What does liability insurance really pay for?

    AXA data (2017-2021): this is how personal liability claims break down.

    51%
    Damage to rented property
    More than half of all liability claims are for damage in a rented flat.
    Examples: a hole drilled in the wrong place, water on the parquet, a burn mark on the worktop, a scratched floor when moving out
    35%
    Other people's vehicles
    Damage to vehicles you drive (not your own car).
    Examples: a parking dent in a hire car, a dent in the company car, a scratch while parking a friend's car
    7%
    Children
    Children under 18 are covered by their parents' liability insurance.
    Examples: a ball through the neighbour's window, a scratch on a parked car, an accident at the playground
    2%
    Animals
    Damage caused by pets. Note: in many cantons, dogs need separate dog owner's liability insurance.
    Examples: a dog bites a passer-by, a cat scratches the neighbour's sofa
    5%
    Other
    Sports and leisure accidents, damage caused while doing someone a favour, and other liability claims.
    ⚠️
    This can get expensive
    Personal injury claims cost CHF 3'500 on average, 4x more than property damage. The largest known single claim: over CHF 2m (a flat fire). Without liability insurance, you pay for everything out of your own pocket. The premium? From CHF 77.60 a year in our test, about CHF 6.50 a month.

    Frequently asked questions

    What you need to know

    Is household contents insurance compulsory in Switzerland? +

    In most cantons, household contents insurance is voluntary. Only a few cantons have an obligation for buildings insurance (this affects owners, not tenants). Personal liability insurance is not compulsory anywhere, but with claims of CHF 2m and more, you definitely want to have it.

    How much does household contents + liability insurance cost in Switzerland? +

    For a rented 3.5-room flat in Zurich with CHF 85'000 of contents cover, household contents and personal liability together cost between CHF 230.58 and 376.60 a year on 29.10.2026, taking each insurer's cheapest package. That is about CHF 19 to 31 a month. Contents alone cost CHF 139 to 295, liability alone CHF 78 to 92. The full table with every package is above.

    How is the household insurance premium calculated? +

    The premium depends on the sum insured, the excess, the add-ons, where you live and the liability limit. Add-ons matter most: at Mobiliar, the same flat costs CHF 230.58 in the Minima package and CHF 520.91 in the Classic package. Taxes such as the 5% federal stamp duty come on top.

    Which household insurance is the best? +

    It depends on what the package includes, so only compare at the same excess. With a CHF 200 excess and CHF 5m liability, Smile was cheapest in our test on 29.10.2026 at CHF 296.40 a year. Generali cost CHF 376.60 but already included theft away from home and accidental damage. The cheapest basic cover came from Mobiliar (Minima, CHF 230.58) with a CHF 400 excess.

    How high should the sum insured be? +

    Rule of thumb: CHF 1'000 per square metre of living space. So for an 80 m² flat, that is CHF 80'000. Note: in 1 in 3 claims, the insurer finds underinsurance, so it is better to aim a little higher. Use our calculator above for a personal recommendation.

    What is the difference between household contents and liability insurance? +

    Household contents = your belongings (furniture, electronics, clothing) in case of fire, water or burglary. Liability = when you cause damage to others (damage to rented property, accidents, children). Most insurers offer both combined, which is usually cheaper than buying them separately.

    When can I cancel my household contents insurance? +

    Many policies run for one year and renew automatically, though some calculators suggest 2 or 5 years. You cancel in writing 3 months before the policy ends. Since 2022, any contract can be cancelled at the end of its third year at the latest. After a claim, you also have a right to cancel.

    Do I really need glass breakage cover? +

    Glass breakage accounts for ~20% of claims, but the amounts are mostly small. It is mainly worth it if you have: a lot of glass (conservatory), an expensive ceramic hob, a high-end shower enclosure. For a standard flat it is usually unnecessary, and you are better off saving that money on the premium.

    Better insured, paying less

    For the same flat, the basic packages were CHF 146 a year apart in our test. Comparing pays off.
