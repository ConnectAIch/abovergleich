    Liability in detail

    What does liability insurance really pay for?

    AXA data (2017-2021): this is how personal liability claims break down.

    51%
    Damage to rented property
    More than half of all liability claims are for damage in a rented flat.
    Examples: a hole drilled in the wrong place, water on the parquet, a burn mark on the worktop, a scratched floor when moving out
    35%
    Other people's vehicles
    Damage to vehicles you drive (not your own car).
    Examples: a parking dent in a hire car, a dent in the company car, a scratch while parking a friend's car
    7%
    Children
    Children under 18 are covered by their parents' liability insurance.
    Examples: a ball through the neighbour's window, a scratch on a parked car, an accident at the playground
    2%
    Animals
    Damage caused by pets. Note: in many cantons, dogs need separate dog owner's liability insurance.
    Examples: a dog bites a passer-by, a cat scratches the neighbour's sofa
    5%
    Other
    Sports and leisure accidents, damage caused while doing someone a favour, and other liability claims.
    ⚠️
    This can get expensive
    Personal injury claims cost CHF 3'500 on average, 4x more than property damage. The largest known single claim: over CHF 2m (a flat fire). Without liability insurance, you pay for everything out of your own pocket. The premium? From CHF 5/month.

    Frequently asked questions

    What you need to know

    Is household contents insurance compulsory in Switzerland? +

    In most cantons, household contents insurance is voluntary. Only a few cantons have an obligation for buildings insurance (this affects owners, not tenants). Personal liability insurance is not compulsory anywhere, but with claims of CHF 2m and more, you definitely want to have it.

    How much does household contents + liability insurance cost in Switzerland? +

    A combined policy costs between CHF 150 and CHF 700 a year, depending on cover and where you live. For a 3.5-room flat with CHF 80'000 of cover, the average is around CHF 250-400/year. Online insurers such as Smile are noticeably cheaper than traditional providers.

    How high should the sum insured be? +

    Rule of thumb: CHF 1'000 per square metre of living space. So for an 80 m² flat, that is CHF 80'000. Note: in 1 in 3 claims, the insurer finds underinsurance, so it is better to aim a little higher. Use our calculator above for a personal recommendation.

    What is the difference between household contents and liability insurance? +

    Household contents = your belongings (furniture, electronics, clothing) in case of fire, water or burglary. Liability = when you cause damage to others (damage to rented property, accidents, children). Most insurers offer both combined, which is usually cheaper than buying them separately.

    When can I cancel my household contents insurance? +

    Most policies run for 1 year and renew automatically. You can cancel in writing 3 months before the policy ends. After a claim, you have a special right to cancel. If you move house, you can also switch early.

    Do I really need glass breakage cover? +

    Glass breakage accounts for ~20% of claims, but the amounts are mostly small. It is mainly worth it if you have: a lot of glass (conservatory), an expensive ceramic hob, a high-end shower enclosure. For a standard flat it is usually unnecessary, and you are better off saving that money on the premium.
