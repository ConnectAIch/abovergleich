Transparency

We've had enough of opaque comparison sites.

Our methodology. How and why we compare.

We just wanted to switch health insurer. So we did what everyone does: visited a comparison site, entered our details, looked at the result.

Then we made the mistake of entering our phone number.

What followed: calls. Not one, not two. Weeks of calls. Different numbers, different brokers, always the same routine. "We have a great offer for you." We told them five times to stop bothering us. It changed nothing. Today, months later, the calls still keep coming.

At some point we asked ourselves: why is that? Why can't a comparison site just compare, without pestering you afterwards?

The answer lies in the business model.

Why do the brokers call?

On 14 August 2024, the Federal Council declared the industry agreement on brokers in health insurance generally binding. Since 1 September 2024, new rules have applied:

The new rules since September 2024
  • Basic health insurance: broker commission capped at CHF 70 per policy sold (santésuisse press release)
  • Cold calling banned: no more unsolicited phone calls
  • Advice records: every consultation must be documented
  • Fines: up to CHF 100'000 for breaches

CHF 70 per policy. That sounds like the end of the broker business. But this is where it gets interesting.

The real money is in supplementary insurance

For supplementary insurance there is also a cap, but a much higher one: up to 16 monthly premiums per policy. More on this in the article Why your adviser wants to sell you supplementary insurance.

Worked example

A typical supplementary policy (hospital, dental, alternative medicine) costs CHF 150 per month.

At 16 monthly premiums, the broker earns on a single policy: CHF 2'400.

That is more than your entire first year of supplementary insurance. Source: Federal Council, press release of 14/08/2024, industry agreement on brokers.

Before the 2024 cap, some insurers added so-called "super commissions". These were bonus schemes that paid brokers 40 to 60% extra on top of the base commission if they delivered enough policies. (SRF Kassensturz investigation) Today such bonuses count towards the cap of 16 monthly premiums.

Estimates from before the cap put broker commissions in health insurance at around CHF 480 million a year. According to the FOPH, only about CHF 59 million of that relates to basic health insurance. The bulk of the rest, an estimated CHF 430 million, goes to supplementary insurance. (watson.ch, 2023) With the CHF 70 cap in place since 2024, basic insurance has become even less attractive for brokers. The money is now in supplementary cover more than ever.

That's why brokers try on every call to talk you into a complete switch. Basic and supplementary insurance together. Basic insurance is the bait.

And the cold-calling ban?

Banned since September 2024. Even so, by February 2026 358 complaints had reached FINMA and 67 the FOPH (prio.swiss, review of February 2026).

The law is in place. Enforcement is lagging behind.

That's why we deliberately don't ask for a phone number

So that we can give you all these comparisons and research without bothering you, we deliberately don't ask for your phone number. We don't pass your data on to any broker. Full stop.

You enter your postcode and year of birth. We show you the cheapest insurers. Done. No catch, no small print.

How we fund ourselves (to be honest)

At the moment we receive no commissions from health insurers. We reserve the right to do so in future via partner links, and would then say so openly here and on every page.

But you can also just use the site to inform yourself. What matters to us is having all the information condensed and easy to understand in one place. Whether you then switch or not is your decision.

Important

Affiliate links never influence the ranking. The order is always: cheapest first. Whether an insurer has a partner programme or not.

Are we a broker?

No. We are currently not an insurance intermediary. We do not broker insurance and do not take commissions.

If we were to receive affiliate commissions in future, we would have to register with FINMA as an insurance intermediary. The Federal Administrative Court made this clear in July 2024 in the Comparis case (ruling B-5886/2023): comparison sites that receive commissions also count as intermediaries and must register. Comparis had previously outsourced the brokering to a subsidiary, and the court did not accept this.

We will say so openly when the time comes. For us, transparency is not a marketing slogan.

Basic health insurance is identical at every insurer

This is the most important fact that many people don't know: the benefits of basic health insurance are set by law (Health Insurance Act, KVG Art. 25-31). Every insurer must cover exactly the same treatments, medicines and hospitals. Whether CSS, Helsana or Assura.

The only difference is the price. And customer service. That's why comparing pays off so much: you get the same cover for less money.
