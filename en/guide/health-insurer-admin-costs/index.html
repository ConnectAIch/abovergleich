Background

What health insurers spend on administration, advertising and brokers

Basic insurance · FOPH figures · reading time: 4 min

On average, administering basic insurance costs CHF 193 per insured person per year (2024), around 5 % of the premium. Behind that are big differences: from CHF 129 at sodalis to CHF 285 at Atupri. In 2025, all insurers together spent CHF 74m on advertising and CHF 33m on broker commissions.

Advertising and brokers

Atupri spends the most on advertising per head: CHF 15 per insured person (2025). For broker commissions ÖKK leads, with CHF 18 per head. Practically no commissions, under CHF 2 per head two years running: Aquilana, Concordia, sodalis and Swica.

In basic insurance, commission is capped: at most CHF 70 per policy, binding on all insurers since 1 September 2024. This shows in the figures:

Who has grown since 2018 and who saves

The largest increases in administrative costs per head: Atupri (+111 %), Assura (+80 %), sana24 (+76 %). Reduced them: EGK (−52 %), Helsana (−32 %), KPT (−9 %). The average of all insurers rose from CHF 168 to CHF 193.

Administration and reserves together

Reserves are the cushion an insurer uses to absorb expensive years without raising premiums straight away. Above-average administrative costs with a solvency ratio below 130 %: Avenir, ÖKK, Assura, sana24. That does not mean anything is wrong, but this is where there is least room for manoeuvre. Reserves count for 10 % of the price consistency score; administrative costs do not, because they are already in the price.

What does this mean for you?

Administration is only a small part of the premium, around 5 %. Between the leanest and the most expensive large insurer the gap is CHF 156 a year. Premium differences are usually much bigger. What matters is what you pay and whether the insurer stays cheap over the years.
